How to Build a Resource Stack for a New Sales Coach Starting From Scratch

Every few weeks someone emails us with a version of the same question. They have just taken on their first sales coaching role, or they are going independent after years on the inside, and they want to know what they actually need to get started. Not the ideal setup for a ten-person coaching practice running at full capacity. The real starting point, for someone who needs to be useful quickly without overspending.

The honest answer is that you need less than you think, at least in the beginning. The temptation when starting out is to build the full professional setup immediately: the premium CRM, the conversation intelligence tool, the fancy assessment platform. Most of that can wait. What cannot wait is having clear visibility into your clients deals, a reliable way to capture call notes, and a small library of frameworks you can actually teach.

For visibility, a simple shared pipeline tracker works well until you have enough clients to justify a proper CRM. We have seen coaches run effective practices for the first year on a well-structured spreadsheet and a good note-taking habit. When you do move to a CRM, our review section has a dedicated guide for solo coaches that flags which platforms are genuinely designed for a one or two person operation and which ones assume you have an operations team behind you.

For frameworks, we recommend starting with two or three books that give you a foundation in discovery, objection handling, and deal review, rather than trying to absorb everything at once. Our beginner reading roadmap maps out a practical six-month reading sequence that builds each skill set on the previous one. Several coaches have told us they used it as the foundation for their own client onboarding reading lists.

The most important thing we tell new coaches is to resist the pressure to have all the answers immediately. The coaches who build lasting practices are the ones who invest in getting sharper, not in looking sophisticated. Build the resource stack that makes you better at coaching first. The tools that make you look more professional can come later.
